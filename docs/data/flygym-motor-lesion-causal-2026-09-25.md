# FlyGym motor-population lesion control — 2026-09-25

The objective remains autonomous, learned food seeking and threat avoidance
generated by the retained MaleCNS pathway. This one-seed intervention does
**not** establish that objective. It tests whether the previously observed
thorax–coxa antagonist coactivation is causally relevant to physical motion.

## Protocol

Source revision `93e2bd82535647137788d3d44b7af5411ded7af0`; retained
166,606-neuron MaleCNS snapshot; FlyGym 2.1.0 body; seed 7; 100 body steps;
the same sensory field, neural parameters, plasticity and initial body state
in all conditions. The `--motor-lesion-group` option silences exactly named,
registry-resolved motor neurons in the Shiu network; it is an explicit
experimental lesion, not a gait policy or desired-action channel. The
`--motor-trace` option records the 36 activations, decoded and applied
18-joint moments, and body response after each step. All conditions replayed
exactly and left the canonical graph unchanged.

The conditions are: no lesion; all six thorax–coxa posterior populations
silenced; all six thorax–coxa anterior populations silenced; and all 36 motor
populations silenced. The last condition delivered exactly zero moment on
every joint and measures passive FlyGym settling. Every lesion name is
serialized in its artifact. Reproduce, for example, the baseline with:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=src .venv/bin/flybrain experiment autonomous-hexapod \
  artifacts/male-cns-v1.0-w5 --steps 100 --seed 7 --backend flygym \
  --motor-trace \
  --output artifacts/autonomous-hexapod-flygym-lesion-93e2bd8-seed7-steps100-baseline.json
```

For lesion conditions, add a repeatable `--motor-lesion-group <name>` for
each population listed in the result's `episode.motor_lesion_groups`.

## Observations

All distances below are to the same food location; the food-contact radius
is 0.200 mm. Distance after step 20 separates early settling from later
motion. “Fell” is an instantaneous simulator flag; all bodies ended supported,
but the normal body also flagged a transient fall at step 40.

| Condition | Motor spikes | Food distance, step 20 | Food distance, step 100 | Food contacts | Final support |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| No lesion | 2,755 | 0.934 mm | 0.939 mm | 0 | 5 legs |
| Posterior lesion | 2,243 | 0.970 mm | 0.885 mm | 0 | 5 legs |
| Anterior lesion | 2,304 | 0.898 mm | 0.812 mm | 0 | 6 legs |
| All motor populations silenced | 0 | 0.903 mm | 0.901 mm | 0 | 6 legs |

From step 20 to 100, the no-lesion thorax moved -0.0103 mm in x, whereas
posterior and anterior lesions moved +0.0920 and +0.1059 mm in x. The
all-motor control moved only +0.00185 mm in x. Thus the two partial lesions
produced physical changes beyond passive settling in this deterministic
episode. Both opposite lesions improved final food distance relative to the
unlesioned condition, so the result does **not** isolate antagonist
cancellation as the unique root cause. Neither lesion is an acceptable
substitute for a naturally functioning motor circuit, and neither contacted
food or demonstrated learning.

The new no-lesion run exactly matched the older 100-step neural episode on
both complete distance traces, final body, motor-spike count, active-group
count and contacts. This checks that the default lesion-free path did not
alter behavior.

## Next gate

The architecture still requires a validated neuron-to-muscle-to-joint bridge
for all relevant leg DOFs and an internally generated, coordinated gait.
Before attributing a behavioral gain to learning, repeat matched food and
threat trials over independent seeds and sufficiently long horizons, with
no-plasticity, DAN-lesion, KC-to-MBON-lesion, rewired and passive controls.
The present one-seed lesion effect is a mechanism diagnostic only.
